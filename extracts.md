# Library extracts: all 30 written sources

Extract types: **verbatim** (word-for-word from a public-domain edition), **our translation** (translated for this project from the original-language text), **one-line quote + retelling**, **retelling** (no quotation; must be labelled as such in the app). `[...]` marks a cut.

## W01 · Boethius: The Consolation of Philosophy, Book I
*c. 524 CE · Pavia, Italy (in prison)* · core feelings: drained, small, wound_up · intensity 3

**Source:** trans. H. R. James (1897), Project Gutenberg #14328, Book I, Song I (opening lines); Prose I; Prose II · **Type:** verbatim

> Who wrought my studious numbers
> Smoothly once in happier days,
> Now perforce in tears and sadness
> Learn a mournful strain to raise.
> Lo, the Muses, grief-dishevelled,
> Guide my pen and voice my woe;
> Down their cheeks unfeigned the tear drops
> To my sad complainings flow!
>
> [...] While I was thus mutely pondering within myself, and recording my sorrowful complainings with my pen, it seemed to me that there appeared above my head a woman of a countenance exceeding venerable. Her eyes were bright as fire, and of a more than human keenness. [...]
>
> 'There is no danger; these are the symptoms of lethargy, the usual sickness of deluded minds. For awhile he has forgotten himself; he will easily recover his memory, if only he first recognises me. And that he may do so, let me now wipe his eyes that are clouded with a mist of mortal things.' Thereat, with a fold of her robe, she dried my eyes all swimming with tears.

## W02 · Seneca the Younger: Moral Letters to Lucilius, Letter 28 ('On Travel as a Cure for Discontent')
*c. 63-65 CE · Rome (born in Corduba, Hispania)* · core feelings: drained, on_edge · intensity 1

**Source:** Translated for this project from the Latin (Epistulae Morales 28.1-2), Letter 28, sections 1-2 · **Type:** our translation

> Do you think this has happened to you alone? Are you surprised, as if it were something new, that after such a long journey and so many changes of scene you still haven't shaken off the gloom and heaviness in your mind? You need to change your soul, not your sky. [...] The same thing that drove you away is still pressing on you. What good can new lands do? Or getting to know new cities and places? All that rushing about comes to nothing. You ask why running away isn't helping? You are taking yourself with you.

**Original:** Hoc tibi soli putas accidisse et admiraris quasi rem novam, quod peregrinatione tam longa et tot locorum varietatibus non discussisti tristitiam gravitatemque mentis? Animum debes mutare, non caelum. [...] Premit te eadem causa, quae expulit. Quid terrarum iuvare novitas potest? Quid cognitio urbium aut locorum? In inritum cedit ista iactatio. Quaeris quare te fuga ista non adiuvet? Tecum fugis.

**Note:** Translation is ours, so no rights issue. Latin text checked against two online transcriptions.

## W04 · Christine de Pizan: The Book of the City of Ladies, opening chapters
*1405 · Paris, France (born in Venice)* · core feelings: small, wound_up · intensity 1

> [A retelling, not a quotation] Christine is sitting in her study, surrounded by books, when she picks up a volume that insults women. She can't stop thinking about it. ## W05 · John Keats: Journal-letter to George and Georgiana Keats ('the vale of Soul-making')
*21 April 1819 (part of a letter written Feb-May 1819) · Hampstead, London* · core feelings: heavy, on_edge, small · intensity 2

> The common cognomen of this world among the misguided and superstitious is "a vale of tears," from which we are to be redeemed by a certain arbitrary interposition of God and taken to Heaven--What a little circumscribed straightened notion! ## W12 · Li Qingzhao: Afterword to Records on Metal and Stone
*c. 1132-1134 · Southern China (as a refugee)* · core feelings: drained, heavy · intensity 3

**Source:** Translated for this project from the Chinese original (金石錄後序, 1132), text via Chinese Wikisource, Closing section of the Afterword · **Type:** our translation

> Today I suddenly opened this book again, and it was like meeting an old friend. I remembered him in the hall at Donglai, the scrolls just newly mounted [...] Every evening, once the clerks had gone home, he would check two scrolls and write a note on one. [...] Now his handwriting looks as fresh as ever, yet the trees on his grave have already grown thick. How sad! [...] How hard these things were to gather, and how easily they were lost! Ah, from when I was eighteen until now, at fifty-two, in thirty-four years, how much worry and sorrow, how much gained and lost! Yet where there is having there must be losing; what gathers together must scatter. That is the ordinary way of things. Someone loses a bow, someone else finds it; why make so much of it?

**Original:** 今日忽閱此書，如見故人。因憶侯在東萊靜治堂，裝卷初就……每日晚，吏散，輒校勘二卷，跋題一卷。……今手澤如新，而墓木已拱，悲夫﹗……何得之艱而失之易也﹗嗚呼﹗余自少陸機作賦之二年，至過蘧瑗知非之兩歲，三十四年之間，憂患得失，何其多也﹗然有有必有無，有聚必有散，乃理之常。人亡弓，人得之，又胡足道？

**Note:** Ages follow the classical allusions in the text (Lu Ji at 20, minus two; Qu Boyu at 50, plus two).

## W13 · Tao Yuanming (Tao Qian): 'Return Home!' (Gui qu lai xi ci), with preface
*405 CE · Pengze county, Jiangxi, China* · core feelings: drained, heavy · intensity 1

**Source:** Translated for this project from the Chinese original (歸去來兮辭并序, 405 CE), text via Chinese Wikisource, Prose preface, and the opening lines of the rhapsody · **Type:** our translation

> My family was poor, and farming could not feed us. The house was full of small children and there was no grain in the jar. [...] So when a post came up in Pengze, a hundred li from home, I took it. But within a few days I felt a longing to go home. Why? My nature is what it is; it cannot be forced into another shape. Hunger and cold are real enough, but going against myself made me sick in body and mind. [...]
>
> Return home! The fields and garden will soon be overgrown; why not go back?
> I have made my mind the servant of my body; why be miserable and grieve alone?
> I see now the past can't be put right, but I know the future can still be reached.
> I have not gone so far down the wrong road; I can see today is right and yesterday was wrong.

**Original:** 余家貧，耕植不足以自給。幼稚盈室，缾無儲粟……及少日，眷然有歸歟之情。何則？質性自然，非矯勵所得。飢凍雖切，違己交病。……歸去來兮，田園將蕪胡不歸？既自以心為形役，奚惆悵而獨悲！悟已往之不諫，知來者之可追；實迷途其未遠，覺今是而昨非。
